magicbar · drain estimate

From one integer to “about two days left”

macOS hands this app a single whole number per device. No capacity, no current, no history. Here is every step between that number and the phrase in the menu bar, worked on the real readings taken from one Magic Mouse and one Magic Keyboard.

00

What the system actually gives

Every five seconds the app reads the IO registry entry for each peripheral that reports a battery. This is the whole payload it has to work with:

$ ioreg -r -c AppleDeviceManagementHIDEventService -l
  "DeviceAddress"      = "bc-89-a7-e3-b9-51"
  "BatteryPercent"     = 14
  "BatteryStatusFlags" = 0
  "HasBattery"         = Yes

There is no milliamp-hour figure, no cycle count, no maximum capacity and no timestamp — those exist elsewhere in the registry only for the Mac’s own battery. A reading is therefore a pair: a whole percent, and the moment the app saw it. Everything below is built from pairs like that.

01

Keep only the moments the number moves

A five-second poll produces 17,280 readings per device per day. A Magic Mouse crosses a whole percent every few hours, so nearly all of those are the previous number again. The app writes a row only when the value actually changes.

Compression, not sampling. The dots above are the complete record: rows for both devices, bytes on disk. Recording every poll instead would have produced roughly rows over the same period, describing exactly the same curve.

02

Decide what each new reading means

A changed value is not automatically drain. It might be a charge, a cable, or Bluetooth noise. Each reading is classified before it is stored:

same as lastDiscard. Nothing happened worth a row.
charging flag setClose the current run. The battery is filling; that is not a drain curve.
rose by 5 or moreTreat as a charge the flag missed. Close the run, open a new one.
rose by 1 to 4Keep it. Bluetooth readings wobble; a wobble is not a cable.
fell by any amountAppend to the current run.

A run of readings between two charges is called a segment. Both devices currently hold exactly one, because neither has been charged since 13 September. Segments are kept, not deleted, when a charge happens — the reason appears in step 04.

03

Convert rows into coordinates

The fit needs numbers, not timestamps. Each row becomes a point: hours elapsed since its segment began, against the level. One extra point is added at the end — now, at the current reading — so that quiet time since the last change counts as time that used almost nothing.

Without that final point, a battery that dropped on Friday and sat untouched all weekend would be measured as though the clock stopped on Friday, and the drain would be overstated several times over.

04

Fit one line through all of it

The rate is the slope of the straight line that sits closest to every point at once — least squares. Two readings alone would let a single wobble set the answer; a line through thirty-three cannot be moved far by any one of them.

rate = − Σ(h−h̄)(L−L̄) ÷ Σ(h−h̄)²

In words: measure how far each point sits from the average time and the average level, multiply those two distances together, and add up the results. Points that are early and high, or late and low, contribute negative numbers — the signature of a falling battery. Dividing by the spread of the times converts “these move together” into “percent per hour”.

Every segment shares one slope, but keeps its own starting level. Each run is centred on its own averages before the sums are combined, so the jump upward at a charge never enters the arithmetic as drain, and time spent on the cable counts toward nothing. Your habits carry across a charge even though the battery level does not — which is why charging no longer blanks the estimate.

05

Not every reading counts equally

The denominator, (h−h̄)², is also the weight each point carries. It grows with the square of a point’s distance from the average time, so the readings at the two ends of a run decide the tilt and the ones in the middle barely register.

06

Refuse to answer too early, then say it plainly

A slope exists as soon as there are two points. It is worth showing much later. Three gates stand between the arithmetic and the menu bar:

≥ 3 pointsTwo points describe a wobble, not a habit.
≥ 24 hoursA shorter window measures a time of day. Working hours read fast; a night reads slow.
slope is fallingA flat or rising fit is not a drain. Silence is the correct output.

Past all three, the level is divided by the rate and the result is rounded into deliberately coarse language. A straight line through whole-number readings cannot justify “2.7 days”.

07

What the residuals admit

A straight line through a battery is an assumption, and the leftovers show where it is wrong. Each point below is one reading minus what the line predicted for that moment.

08

Known weaknesses

post-charge creepThe gauge settles upward for a while after unplugging. Those readings sit at the high-influence end of the run and tilt the line. Uncorrected.
habit changeA quiet week and a failing battery look identical from the outside. This is why the app does not claim to report battery health.
whole-number readingsAt one percent a day, a device produces one data point a day. Precision arrives slowly.
straight-line assumptionThe residuals arc rather than scatter. Nothing yet suggests a faster drain near empty, but the record only reaches 14%.
